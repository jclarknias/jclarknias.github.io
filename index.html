Brave I.D.E.A.S. Lab

Identity, Data, Ethics, AI, and Society

Mission

The Brave IDEAS Lab designs and develops interaction technologies and practices that promote socially responsible and culturally meaningful computing artifacts. Our work centers culture as a technical foundation, advancing human-centered AI systems grounded in identity, language, and community knowledge.

Projects

2026

CARE: Culturally Aligned AI for Reflective Creativity

Students: Kamili Campbell, Janelle Yankey, Thane Douglass • AAAI Spring Symposium 2026

CARE is a culturally grounded generative AI system that supports creative reflection through symbolic mediation. Drawing on Adinkra symbols and proverb-based reasoning, the system positions AI as a partner in sensemaking rather than a prescriptive tool. This work demonstrates how cultural knowledge systems can be embedded into AI design to support meaningful and context-aware creativity.

Symbolic Mediation for Tactical Decision Support

Student: Lashaun Baddol • AAAI 2026 Spring Symposium

This project introduces a neuro-symbolic framework that transforms language model outputs into structured operational symbols for decision-making contexts. By pairing textual explanations with symbolic representations, the system supports human-centered interpretation in time-sensitive environments.

2025

Haptic Feedback for Tonal Language Learning

Student: Kamili Campbell • NSEA 2025 • Howard Research Month

This project explores the use of haptic feedback to support tonal language learning. Using a game-based interface, users receive vibrational cues aligned with pitch variation, enabling a multi-sensory approach to learning languages such as Yoruba.

CROSS AWARE: Pedestrian Distraction and Safety

Students: Kamili Campbell, Sarah Kabbo • DCQI 2025

CROSS AWARE is a smartwatch-based system for studying pedestrian distraction and group dynamics. The platform captures in-the-moment behavioral reflections and integrates wearable alerts to promote safer navigation in high-traffic environments.

AAVE Slang in Large Language Models

Students: Thane Douglass, Ashley Hanes • AfriCHI 2025

This project evaluates how large language models interpret African American Vernacular English (AAVE). Findings highlight challenges in cultural context, nuance, and linguistic representation, emphasizing the need for more inclusive AI development practices.

AfroComputation Praxis in Sentiment Analysis

Students: Kaylin Clay, Myla Williams, Kamili Campbell • AfriCHI 2025

This work applies AfroComputation Praxis to develop a culturally fluent sentiment lexicon for AAVE. The project demonstrates how culturally grounded approaches can reshape computational tools and challenge dominant assumptions in language processing.

Àṣẹ Across the Diaspora

Student: Thane Douglass • AfriCHI 2025

This case study examines the layered meanings of Àṣẹ across diasporic contexts and explores how computational systems can better represent culturally embedded concepts through contextual annotation and retrieval-based approaches.

AfroComputation Praxis in Computing Education

Paper: BICE 2025

This project presents a culturally grounded data science curriculum that integrates AfroComputation Praxis into computing education. It highlights how identity, culture, and technical skill-building can be meaningfully aligned.
